How the rock works

By ZyroX. I make this game by myself. September 2026.

Corez.io is a tank battle royale where the whole map is rock. You shoot the rock to dig out gems, but if you die you drop everything you're carrying, so you have to get to a vault and bank it before somebody kills you for it.

I didn't start from zero. The base is Open Source Arras, the community remake of arras.io: a Node server, raw WebSockets, and a canvas client. The tanks, bullets and physics are theirs. The rock, mining, banking, storm, bots, bosses, shop, growth, contracts, ranked, duos and tutorial are what I added on top. Big thanks to the Open Source Arras people, none of this happens without them.

This page is for anyone curious how some of it works. It's the stuff that broke on me and how I fixed it.

The rock

The map is a grid of points, and each point gets pushed a bit off center by a hash. Every rock is the Voronoi cell around one of those points, which is why they look like broken stone instead of squares. The map always ends up about 50 rocks wide no matter how big it is.

Four copies that have to match

This was the hardest part. The same rock layout lives in four places: the server's collision code, the client's rock outline, a debug view, and a shader that draws the rock faces. If even one point is off, you get a rock you can drive through or a wall you can't see.

And that's exactly what happened. One or two rocks per map were drawn in the wrong place. It turned out the GPU was doing its floating point math slightly differently (fused multiply-add) right where the hash wraps around, so a few points moved. I gave up on letting the GPU do the hash. Now the server does it in float32 with Math.fround at every step, the client does the same thing on the CPU, and it hands the shader a small texture with the answers. They match exactly now.

Ore has its own hash that gets a new salt every time the server starts, so the gems are in different spots each run but every player sees the same ones.

The rock shapes are never sent over the network. Your browser builds them itself from a few numbers, and after that the server only tells you which rocks got hit or broke.

Bullets and regrowth

If a bullet hits right on the line between two rocks it damages both, but it still only counts as one hit. Every tank also has a limit on how fast it can mine, otherwise a tank with eight guns just melts walls.

Broken rock grows back. About 46 seconds after a rock breaks it starts growing again, and it takes 16 seconds to fill in. If a tank is standing close it grows a bit slower, so you have time to get out. If you're still in there when it finishes, it pushes you out.

The server

The game runs 30 ticks a second in Node. The first raids stuttered even though the server was barely working. Two things were wrong: the terrain had its own timer that kept fighting the main loop, and the timestamps I sent to clients came straight from the clock, so they jumped around a lot. Clients use those timestamps to smooth movement, so everything looked jittery.

I moved terrain into the main loop and made the timestamps tick evenly, only slowly correcting toward the real time. The jitter went from about 11 ms to under 1 ms. A tick takes 2 to 4 ms on the live server now.

Players far away

Messages get compressed, which roughly halves them. What helped more for people with bad connections was skipping old updates. Each update is the full state of what you can see, so an old one is useless once a newer one exists. The client confirms every update it gets, and if they start piling up the server skips one instead of adding to the pile.

Bots

Bots fill in when there aren't enough people. The first thing I learned is that smooth movement gives a bot away in seconds, so bots move like they're pressing keys: eight directions, full speed or stopped, and they let go of keys at slightly random times. Some of them even zone out for a second.

They go easier on new players

My stats showed new players dying to bots in under a minute and leaving. So now every bot checks who it's fighting. If you're new, bots are easy for your first few minutes. If you play ranked, bots match your rank. Guests get tougher bots the longer and better they play, but never all the way. Aim, reaction time, damage, and how many bots will go after you at once all come from that.

If bots kill you three times in a row quickly, they leave you alone for 30 seconds. That only works if you're actually playing, though. Someone found out you could stand still behind the spawn shield forever, so now if you don't touch anything for a minute the protection is gone, and after 5 minutes you're sent back to the menu.

Slow computers

One player told me they were getting 6 fps. Their browser was running WebGL on the CPU, and my rock shader took about 220 ms per frame by itself. The rock doesn't move, so now I draw it once into a bigger image and just slide that around as you move. On that kind of setup frames went from 236 ms to 16 ms.

Small freezes after that were the garbage collector. I was measuring text over 2000 times a second, and cutting that down fixed most of it. If the game is still slow it lowers the resolution by itself, and there are settings to turn off effects if you want.

How a raid plays

A raid is 28 minutes, which is four storms of 7 minutes each. The last one closes on a small circle and whoever's left wins. You can join whenever.

Banking is the whole game. You die, you drop your gems. Vaults bank fast, but getting hit stops you, so the last few seconds on a vault are scary. Your tank also gets bigger as your score goes up. Bigger means slower but tougher, and killing a giant pays out.

Contracts give you one small job at a time based on what's near you, like a chest, a boss, a fight, or getting back to the safe zone.

Stuff players taught me

New players would just stand in the storm and not realize they had to move. Now the storm starts weak and gets worse the longer you stay in it, so you get a warning before it gets serious.

Making bots worse on purpose felt like cheating at first. But if a new player never gets to bank anything, they never find out what the game even is.

Go play it

It's free and runs in your browser, no account needed. Keyboard and mouse is best, but it works on phones too if you hold them sideways. Please tell me what confused you in the first minute and what felt bad. I read everything.
